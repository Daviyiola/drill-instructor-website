# ACT Reading source review

## Overall assessment

All 15 source groups are strong enough to guide ACT-ready generation. Each group contains a substantial passage or paired passages and 9 source question stems. Collectively they model the major Reading operations needed by the current module taxonomy: explicit detail, inference, main idea/theme, author perspective, paragraph function, vocabulary in context, sequence/causation, character relationships, and paired-passage comparison. Each source can support two blueprints provided the second blueprint changes both its content domain and its structural/question emphasis rather than merely reskinning the first.

The sources should be used only for architecture. They contain copyrighted attributions and text, printed line numbers, OCR/encoding artifacts, and distinctive content that must not survive into generated assets. The source questions also provide stems without options or answer keys; they are useful for analyzing the reasoning mix but cannot independently establish answer-choice quality.

## Coverage judgment

The set is especially strong for Function and Structure, Inference and Implication, Textual Details, Central Ideas and Themes, and Sequence and Relationships. Sources 1, 4, 9, and 12 provide genuine cross-passage opportunities. Sources 1, 4, 8, and 12 support character and relationship analysis. The nonfiction sources provide enough conceptual density for author-purpose and vocabulary questions without forcing literary modules.

The revised prompt can generate ACT-ready material from these sources if every batch passes passage verification, question verification, and deterministic validation. Prompt quality alone cannot guarantee publishable questions; the final human review remains necessary because nuanced ambiguity and factual plausibility are not fully deterministic.
